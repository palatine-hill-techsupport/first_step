Executive summary

Did the bridge work?

The demonstration suggests that first_step can turn low-pressure public and online contact into real conversations, referrals and paid opportunities. The strongest result is not reach alone. It is the number of people who chose a second step.

Read this first: the figures show a connected service journey, not proven long-term outcomes.

People used both doors

The public pop-up generated visibility. The online channel gave people a quieter way to return later.

Human support mattered

Appointments and warm referrals were the points where a browse became a chosen next step.

The bridge still loses people

A meaningful number of users began a pathway but did not complete it. The next pilot should test shorter flows and stronger follow-up choices.

What the pilot learned

Keep the useful parts. Test the rest.

01

The public activation opened the door

The pop-up made the service visible and normal, especially for people who may not actively search for homelessness support.

02

The quieter second door mattered

Most appointments came from online and return visits rather than at the event itself.

03

Explain what happens next

Resources and booking options performed better when they stated the cost, contact method and likely next step.

04

Follow-up must stay consent-led

Participants valued being able to come back without repeated unsolicited contact.

Credibility boundary

What this pilot cannot tell us yet

These omissions are not a weakness in the report. They are the questions a careful next pilot must answer.

  • Whether outcomes were sustained long term
  • Whether accepted referrals led to stable housing
  • Whether paid placements continued
  • Whether confidence changes lasted
  • Whether the model works outside one local pilot
  • Whether the sponsor model is financially sustainable
  • Whether people who did not engage found the service useful
  • Whether the same outcomes would occur at a larger scale
Privacy and sponsor boundaries

Measure the bridge. Protect the person.

Sponsor funding does not purchase access to participant data.

Sponsors and public partners may see

  • Aggregate engagement totals
  • Grouped pathway interests
  • Conversion rates
  • Anonymised feedback
  • Overall referral outcomes
  • Broad financial reporting

Sponsors and public partners do not see

  • Names
  • Contact details
  • Appointment messages
  • Individual referral records
  • Personal histories
  • Worker notes
  • Identifiable journeys
  • Participant-level dashboards
Next-pilot recommendations

Scale only what proves useful, safe and worth returning to.

  1. 1

    Shorten the gap between pathway completion and booking.

  2. 2

    Test optional text reminders for appointments.

  3. 3

    Add stronger return-later and save-without-an-account options.

  4. 4

    Recruit more partners for housing and paid-work pathways.

  5. 5

    Extend follow-up measurement to three and six months.
