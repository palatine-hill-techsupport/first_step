# Draft pilot-readiness requirements

> **Internal draft — not approval to operate.**
>
> This note records requirements that must be resolved before first_step is used as a live service for young people aged 15–25. It does not confirm that any policy, training, process or partner arrangement is currently in place. Interface changes and automated tests are not substitutes for qualified youth-work, safeguarding, privacy and legal review.

Each requirement needs a named owner, documented evidence, a review date and formal approval from the appropriate youth-work and privacy leads before a pilot begins.

## 1. Formal safeguarding policies and procedures

- Develop a written safeguarding framework for every supported age group, contact format and service location.
- Have qualified youth-work governance review the framework alongside applicable Victorian and Australian legal obligations.
- Define how policies are approved, communicated, reviewed and updated.
- Record how concerns, incidents, complaints and policy exceptions are handled.

**Evidence required before pilot:** approved policy set, named policy owner, version history and review schedule.

## 2. Staff safeguarding training

- Define mandatory induction and refresher training for staff, contractors and volunteers who may interact with young people or their information.
- Include recognising and responding to safeguarding concerns, safe contact, confidentiality limits, record keeping, referrals and immediate-risk procedures.
- Confirm role-specific supervision, scenario practice and competency checks rather than relying on attendance alone.
- Keep auditable training records and a process for addressing expired or incomplete training.

**Evidence required before pilot:** approved training plan, completion records, competency criteria and refresher schedule.

## 3. Defined roles and responsibilities

- Name the people accountable for safeguarding governance, day-to-day decisions, privacy, information security, service operations and partner oversight.
- Define the authority and limits of youth workers, administrators, supervisors and escalation leads.
- Document who is available during service hours and who can make urgent decisions.
- Prevent responsibility gaps across online, phone, text, video and in-person contact.

**Evidence required before pilot:** approved responsibility matrix, current contact roster, delegation rules and escalation cover.

## 4. Confidential record-keeping procedures

- Approve what workers may record, why it is needed, where it is stored and who may access it.
- Keep notes brief, relevant and confidential, with access limited to authorised roles.
- Define retention, correction, access, deletion, audit and secure-disposal procedures.
- Separate participant records from aggregate sponsor or impact reporting.
- Ensure young people receive clear, approved information about notes, consented sharing and confidentiality limits before information is collected.

**Evidence required before pilot:** approved records procedure, access controls, retention schedule, audit process and tested participant-request workflow.

## 5. Risk identification

- Define the safeguarding and operational risks workers are expected to notice without requiring unnecessary disclosure.
- Provide proportionate prompts and decision support for concerns such as immediate danger, unsafe contact, exploitation, family violence, self-harm or harm to others.
- Document when a concern needs consultation, escalation or an external emergency response.
- Review risk-identification guidance with qualified practitioners and people with relevant lived experience.

**Evidence required before pilot:** approved risk framework, decision aids, supervision process and scenario-test results.

## 6. Reporting and escalation pathways

- Document how staff report a concern, incident, near miss, privacy issue or suspected breach.
- Set clear internal escalation levels, response expectations, after-hours boundaries and handover requirements.
- Identify any external reporting obligations through qualified legal and safeguarding review; do not infer them from interface copy.
- Define how actions and decisions are recorded, reviewed and closed.

**Evidence required before pilot:** approved escalation map, reporting forms, contact details, response targets and completed rehearsal.

## 7. Referral-partner responsibilities

- Agree each partner's service scope, eligibility, response times, safeguarding duties and responsibility after a referral is accepted.
- Define the minimum information that may be shared, the secure transfer method and how participant consent is recorded.
- Document what happens when a referral is declined, delayed, cannot make contact or identifies a new risk.
- Establish partner assurance, issue reporting, periodic review and agreement-termination processes.

**Evidence required before pilot:** signed partner agreements, data-sharing terms, referral workflow, escalation contacts and assurance schedule.

## 8. Immediate risk of harm

- Approve a specific procedure for situations where a worker believes a young person or someone else may be at serious and immediate risk of harm.
- Define how the worker confirms essential information, seeks supervision where time permits, contacts the appropriate emergency or specialist service, and records the decision.
- Include procedures for loss of contact, unsafe communication channels, remote appointments and in-person locations.
- Rehearse the procedure across every service format and ensure staff understand that first_step is not an emergency or continuously monitored service.

**Evidence required before pilot:** approved immediate-risk procedure, current emergency contacts, staff rehearsal records and post-incident review process.

## Approval boundary

No pilot should be described as operationally ready until the evidence above has been reviewed and signed off by named accountable leads. Final decisions should also incorporate privacy impact assessment, legal review, under-18 consent and capacity review, accessibility testing with young people, security review, worker screening and supervision, and verified crisis-service information.

This document should be reviewed whenever the service model, contact formats, supported locations, data handling or referral partners change.
